\label{ch:results}

This chapter presents the results of the evaluation of MLguide.
As described in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}, MLguide was
evaluated in two ways, through user testing with students from
the course TMM4128 Machine Learning for Engineers, referred to as Project 1 and Project 2,
and through an
example-based evaluation using a set of constructed problem
cases.

Because MLguide was developed iteratively, the three evaluations
were carried out on different versions of the system, as shown in Section~\ref{sec:development-timeline}.
Project 1 (P1) used version A, Project 2 (P2) used version B, and the example-based
evaluation used version C, the final version. The differences
between the versions are summarised in
Section~\ref{sec:changes-across-system-versions}.

Sections~\ref{sec:project-1} and~\ref{sec:project-2} present the
results from the two user testing projects. 
Section~\ref{sec:results-example-based-evaluation} presents the results
of the example-based evaluation.

Although MLguide includes a feedback form, it produced too few
responses to serve as a usable basis for evaluation. The user
testing results reported here are therefore only from the
students' project reports. Since the reports
were not written to a fixed structure, these results were
obtained by interpreting each group's description of its use of MLguide.

\section{Project 1: Unsupervised Learning and Anomaly Detection for Time Series Data}
\label{sec:project-1}

In the first project, the groups worked on detecting anomalies in
time series data using unsupervised learning. As part of the
assignment, the groups were instructed to try MLguide for their
selected problem, and to assess the
relevance and usefulness of the results in their project reports.
The results below are based on these reports.

Of the 17 groups in the project, 13 used MLguide and four did not,
as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:participation-count-p1}. 
The results that follow are based on the 13 groups that used the system.


\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{Figures/07-results/p1-plots/participation-count.pdf}
     \caption[P1 group participation]{Number of groups in Project 1
    that used MLguide and number that did not.}
    \label{fig:participation-count-p1}
\end{figure}

The reports also indicated when in the project MLguide was used.
Figure~\ref{fig:timing-p1} shows the result. Seven groups used
the system late, after they had already chosen methods, and six
used it early, before choosing methods.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{Figures/07-results/p1-plots/timing.pdf}
    \caption[P1 timing of MLguide use]{Number of groups that used
    MLguide early, before choosing methods, and late, after
    choosing methods.}
    \label{fig:timing-p1}
\end{figure}

For each group, the methods recommended by MLguide were compared
with the methods the group used. One group was excluded because 
its report did not state which methods MLguide recommended, 
leaving twelve groups that could be assessed. The overlap was measured as the
share of the group's chosen methods that MLguide also recommended,
and classified as high (at least 50\%), medium (33\% to $<$50\%), or low
(below 33\%). Figure~\ref{fig:recommendation-overlap-p1} shows the
distribution. Of the twelve groups that could be assessed, six had
high overlap, five had medium overlap, and one had low overlap.

\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:overlap-by-timing-p1} combines the overlap level
with the timing of use. Among the groups that used MLguide early,
three had high overlap, two had medium overlap, and one had low
overlap. Among the groups that used it late, three had high
overlap and three had medium overlap.

\begin{figure}[H]

Figure~\ref{fig:recommended-vs-used-p1} compares, for each
method, the number of groups that were recommended the method
with the number of groups that used it. Some methods were
recommended more often than they were used, such as neural
network, PCA, and LSTM. Others were used more often than they were
recommended, most clearly isolation forest. 
GMM, one-class
SVM, and DBSCAN were used by several groups without being
recommended to any of them.

The reports were also examined for benefits and issues that the

\section{Project 2: Reinforcement Learning Across Product Lifecycle}
\label{sec:project-2}

The second project asked the groups to analyse applications of
reinforcement learning across four product lifecycle phases, and to
implement a simulated RL environment with a sustainability agent. 
The groups were asked to assess the
applicability of MLguide to their project work and to report on it.
The results below are based on the project reports.

Of the 19 groups in the project, 9 used MLguide and 10 did not. The
results that follow are based on the 9 groups that used the system.
Figure~\ref{fig:participation-count-p2} shows the participation in
Project 2 alongside Project 1, where a larger share of the groups
used the system.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{Figures/07-results/p2-plots/participation-p1-p2.pdf}
    \caption[P2 group participation]{Number of groups that used
    MLguide and number that did not, in Project 1 and Project 2.}
    \label{fig:participation-count-p2}
\end{figure}

The reports were examined for how the groups used MLguide.
Figure~\ref{fig:how-groups-used-mlguide-p2} shows the result. Five
groups used only the recommended methods, three used both the
methods and the supporting articles, and for one group the use
was not specified.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{Figures/07-results/p2-plots/how-groups-used-mlguide.pdf}
    \caption[P2 how groups used MLguide]{How the groups used
    MLguide, counted by the number of groups.}
    \label{fig:how-groups-used-mlguide-p2}
\end{figure}

The reports were also examined for benefits that the groups 
raised when describing their use of MLguide. The points were 
sorted into themes, and each theme was counted by the number of 
groups that raised it. Figure~\ref{fig:key-benefits-p2} shows 
the result. Three benefits were each raised by three groups: 
confirmation that the tool was applicable to the problem, help 
with finding or confirming relevant articles, and a good 
overview of relevant reinforcement learning methods. Two groups 
reported that the recommendations were consistent with the 
literature they reviewed for their lifecycle phase cases. One 
group reported that MLguide was sufficient for standard 
single-algorithm reinforcement learning.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{Figures/07-results/p2-plots/key-benefits.pdf}
    \caption[P2 key benefits]{Key benefits reported by groups.}
    \label{fig:key-benefits-p2}
\end{figure}

The reports also raised a small number of issues. First, the 
method ranking was unclear: it was not obvious which 
recommendation to prioritise, and the top-ranked method was not 
always the best fit. Second, the input form did not capture 
hybrid architectures, non-standard states, or deployment 
constraints, which limited the system to standard 
single-algorithm reinforcement learning.

Figure~\ref{fig:methods-used-vs-recommended-p2} compares, for each
method, the number of groups that were recommended the method with
the number of groups that used it. DQN, proximal policy optimization (PPO), and Q-learning were
the most frequently used methods, and were also among the most
frequently recommended. Deep RL and neural network were
recommended without being used by any group. PI-QT-Opt and
NSGA-III were used by one group each without being recommended.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{Figures/07-results/p2-plots/methods-used-vs-recommended.pdf}
    \caption[P2 recommended vs.\ used]{Number of groups recommended
    each method by MLguide compared with the number of groups that
    used it.}
    \label{fig:methods-used-vs-recommended-p2}
\end{figure}

\section{Example-Based Evaluation}
\label{sec:results-example-based-evaluation} 

Three example problems were submitted to MLguide to examine 
the output produced under controlled inputs. The examples cover 
different ML areas and tasks: a supervised classification 
problem, a reinforcement learning problem, and an unsupervised 
anomaly detection problem. They also use different combinations 
of filters, to see how the recommendations change with the level of detail 
provided.

\subsection{Example 1: Supervised Classification}
\label{sec:example-1}

The first example concerns fault classification of a pump 
based on sensor readings. The problem description was 
submitted with ML area set to supervised learning, task set to 
classification, dataset type set to tabular, and performance 
preferences set to fast training and explainable.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{Figures/07-results/example-based/ex-1-problem.pdf}
    \caption[Example 1 problem]{Example 1 problem description and selected filters.}
    \label{fig:results-example-1-problem}
\end{figure}

The recommendations are shown in 
Figure~\ref{fig:recommendations-example-1-rec}. Ten ranked 
methods are returned. The top recommendations include common 
classification methods such as random forest, support vector 
machine, and k-nearest neighbours, as well as neural network, 
which has the highest article count of the recommended methods. 
Gaussian mixture model (GMM) is ranked second, even though it is 
flagged as not suited for the task. Two further methods are 
flagged as not suited for the task: linear regression and 
principal component analysis (PCA). Two methods are flagged as not 
suited for the dataset type: principal component analysis and 
convolutional neural network. Four methods carry additional 
conditions in the Note column, such as being possible if 
numerical features are available or not recommended for many 
features.
Several methods match the performance preference of fast 
training, fewer match the explainable preference, and several 
have no performance preference match at all.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{Figures/07-results/example-based/ex-1-rec.pdf}
    \caption[Example 1 recommendations]{Recommended methods for Example 1.}
    \label{fig:recommendations-example-1-rec}
\end{figure}

The top recommendation, random forest, is mentioned in 3598 
articles within the ML area supervised learning. The top-ranked
articles retrieved for this method are shown in 
Figure~\ref{fig:articles-example-1-rec}. Several of them 
concern pump condition monitoring or fault classification. 
The figure also includes a snippet of the generated notebook, 
scrolled to the model configuration and training steps.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{Figures/07-results/example-based/ex-1-articles-rf.pdf}
    \caption[Example 1 articles]{Top-ranked articles retrieved for random forest in Example 1.}
    \label{fig:articles-example-1-rec}
\end{figure}
